**Availability** is the share of time the system works: `uptime ÷ (uptime + downtime)`. Each extra nine cuts the allowed downtime <mark>10×</mark> (a year has 8,760 hours):

| Availability | Downtime per year | Downtime per day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 99% | 3.65 days | 14.4 min |
| 99.9% | 8.76 hours | 1.44 min |
| 99.99% | 52.6 min | 8.6 s |
| 99.999% | 5.26 min | 0.86 s |

<Interactive name="nines" />

- **Each nine costs far more engineering:** multi-region deployments, automated failover, no maintenance windows. Payments and critical gateways aim for four or five nines.
- **Dependencies multiply:** two 99.9% services called in series give about 99.8%. Redundant copies in parallel raise availability.

## SLI, SLO, SLA

| Term | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SLI | What you measure | Uptime, p99 latency |
| SLO | Your internal target | 99.95% of requests succeed each month |
| SLA | The contractual promise to customers, with penalties | 99.9% or service credits are refunded |

Choosing SLIs, setting targets and spending the error budget: [SLIs, SLOs and error budgets](/learn/slos-error-budgets).

## High availability

**High availability (HA)** means surviving failures without users noticing: no single point of failure, redundant servers behind load balancers, database replicas, several zones or regions, and **graceful degradation** (losing one of ten servers makes things slightly slower, not broken). **Failover** switches to a standby automatically, such as promoting a replica when the primary database dies (see [Database replication](/learn/replication)).

## RPO and RTO

<Diagram name="rpo-rto-timeline" caption="RPO looks back to the last good copy; RTO looks forward to recovery. Not to scale." />

- **RPO (recovery point objective):** how much data you can afford to lose, counted back from the failure to the last good copy (30 s, 1 hour). Asynchronous replication and rare backups widen it; synchronous replication pushes it toward zero.
- **RTO (recovery time objective):** how long you can afford to be down before service returns (5 minutes, 1 hour). Automated failover and warm standbys shrink it.

Backups that actually restore, recovery tiers and losing a whole region: [Disaster recovery and backups](/learn/disaster-recovery).

<Callout type="warning" title="Reality check">

Even the biggest clouds rarely promise five nines; most service SLAs are 99.9–99.99%, and multi-hour cloud outages still happen.

</Callout>

<DeepDive title="Go deeper: error budgets">

Google's SRE practice turns the SLO into a budget. A 99.9% SLO allows 0.1% failure: about 43 minutes in a 30-day month (30 × 1,440 min × 0.001). While budget remains, teams ship freely; once it is spent, releases slow down and reliability work comes first. It turns "how reliable should we be?" into a number both product and operations teams can plan around.

</DeepDive>
