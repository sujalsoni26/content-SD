Computers find each other by **IP address** (`203.0.113.10`); people type names (`instagram.com`). DNS (Domain Name System) is the internet's phone book, mapping <mark>names → IPs</mark>.

## How a lookup works

<Steps>

1. **Check the browser and OS cache.** If you visited recently, you're done.
2. **Ask a resolver**: your ISP's, or a public one like `8.8.8.8`.
3. The **root server** points to the `.com` servers.
4. The **TLD server** (top-level domain, here `.com`) points to the domain's name server.
5. The **authoritative server** returns the IP: an **A record** for IPv4, an **AAAA record** for IPv6.
6. The resolver **caches** the answer for its TTL (time to live).

</Steps>

## Caching cuts both ways

- Caching makes lookups near-instant, but **DNS changes spread slowly** until old TTLs expire. Before a planned migration, teams lower the TTL days in advance.
- DNS can hand **different IPs to different users**, such as the nearest data center. This is <mark>geo-routing</mark>, and CDNs rely on it (see [CDN](/learn/cdn)).
- The same lookup pattern runs **inside** a data center to find services rather than websites (see [Service discovery and internal DNS](/learn/service-discovery)); CDNs push it further with anycast (see [CDN architecture in depth](/learn/cdn-internals)).

You don't memorise phone numbers; you tap a name. If the contact isn't saved, you ask a friend who knows someone who knows. Once you have the number, you save it, and if your friend changes it you keep dialling the old one until you update.

<DeepDive title="Go deeper: common record types">

| Record | Maps | Example use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | name → IPv4 address | `instagram.com → 203.0.113.10` |
| AAAA | name → IPv6 address | The same, for IPv6 |
| CNAME | name → another name | `www.example.com → example.com`, or pointing at a CDN hostname |
| MX | domain → mail servers | Where to deliver email |
| NS | domain → its name servers | How the TLD knows who is authoritative |
| TXT | domain → free text | Domain ownership checks, email anti-spoofing (SPF) |

</DeepDive>
